A determinant is only part of the story

See which matrices are positive semidefinite.

Each point (x, y, z) chooses the three off-diagonal entries of the symmetric matrix below. Its diagonal is fixed at t = 1. Move the probe to see whether that matrix lies inside the teal region, on its boundary, or outside.

M(x,y,z) = [[1,x,y],[x,1,z],[y,z,1]] · det M = 1 + 2xyz − x² − y² − z²

What are these shapes?

A point is a matrix. The axes are its entries, not its eigenvalues. A symmetric matrix scales three perpendicular directions by real numbers called eigenvalues. Positive definite (PD) means all three are positive; positive semidefinite (PSD) allows zero. The teal boundary encloses the PD matrices and is itself PSD.

The surface separates regions. The determinant is the product of the eigenvalues, so its zero surface marks singular matrices with at least one zero eigenvalue. A chamber is a connected region away from that surface: the counts of positive and negative eigenvalues stay constant there. The purple wings are boundary pieces with a negative eigenvalue, not chambers themselves.

        Chambers, the full cone & what FiniteFree computes
        

          Restore the common diagonal t to get M(t,x,y,z) and the homogeneous
          polynomial P(t,x,y,z) = t³ + 2xyz − t(x² + y² + z²). The full closed
          cone consists of the points where this matrix is PSD. Scaling all four
          coordinates by a positive factor stays in that cone. This plot fixes t
          = 1: it is a three-dimensional cross-section of the four-dimensional
          cone, not the whole cone. This section is a spectrahedron, meaning an
          affine matrix-PSD constraint; here it is the set of 3 × 3 correlation
          matrices.
        

        

          The identity matrix at e = (1,0,0,0) lies in the positive-definite
          chamber. Moving along that direction adds sI, so determinant roots are
          negatives of real eigenvalues. This explains hyperbolicity in this
          direction for this particular pencil. Other nonsingular chambers can
          have different eigenvalue signs: the purple filled region in the slice
          has two negative eigenvalues and positive determinant; the gray region
          has one negative eigenvalue and negative determinant. The zero surface
          separates them.
        

        

          In the t = 1 section, PSD is equivalent to all three displayed 2 × 2
          principal minors and the determinant being nonnegative (the 1 × 1
          minors equal 1). On the slice, the boundary satisfies x² + y² − 2zxy =
          1 − z². At |z| = 1 the PSD region in the slice collapses to a line
          segment; at |z| > 1 there is no PSD point on that plane.
        

        

          FiniteFree constructs the exact determinant and substitutes t = 1
          before exporting rational sparse coefficients. Surface meshing,
          polynomial evaluation, eigenvalues and membership labels in this
          offline browser use Float64, with finite resolution and tolerances.
          This display does not certify general hyperbolicity or stability.
          Regenerate with
          PYTHONPATH=. python examples/interactive_geometry.py.
