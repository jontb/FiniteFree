FINITEFREE / Geometry lab02 · Moving line slices
Crossings, coupling & interlacing

Move a line. Watch its roots respond.

The sliders choose a symmetric 4 × 4 matrix A = yD + zB. A separate number s varies in sI + A. Its determinant is a polynomial: the four roots are the values of s that make this matrix singular.

p(s) = P(s,y,z) = det(sI + yD + zB) · D = diag(−3,−1,1,3)

What is moving, and what is a root?

Sliders choose the line; s runs along it. A matrix pencil is a family built from fixed matrices. Here I is the identity, y scales the diagonal D, and z couples neighboring rows through B (ones next to its diagonal, zeros elsewhere). On the line L(s) = (s,y,z), only the polynomial variable x = s changes; y and z stay fixed for that selected polynomial.

Roots are negatives of eigenvalues. If A scales a direction by λ (an eigenvalue), then sI + A scales it by s + λ. It becomes singular at s = −λ. Since A is symmetric, all four eigenvalues and all four roots are real, counting multiplicity. The left panel shows how those root positions change across y; the right panel shows the polynomial at the selected y and z.

Try this in one minute

This links multivariate determinant geometry to real-rooted polynomials: watch how coupling changes root spacing and how the derivative organizes the gaps.

  1. Reset, then Sweep y. The vertical line selects y. Its four colored circles match the roots in the right-hand plot; the three diamonds sit between them.
  2. At the crossing. This sets y = z = 0, giving p(s) = s⁴. All four roots overlap at zero; p′(s) = 4s³ has three zeros there, counting multiplicity.
  3. Raise Coupling z. The roots separate and avoid crossing as y moves. Try Zero coupling and Sweep y to see the crossing return. Reset stops the sweep.
Why coupling opens gaps · interlacing & exact FiniteFree data 


        

          B connects rows 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4. At z = 0 these links disappear:
          roots are −y times −3, −1, 1 and 3, so the four diagonal branches
          cross at y = 0. Their colors follow diagonal identities. At z > 0
          every link is nonzero; this symmetric tridiagonal matrix has distinct
          eigenvalues, so roots cannot meet. These are avoided crossings; colors
          now follow ascending root ranks.
        

        

          Differentiation gives three roots d₁, d₂, d₃ between the four sorted
          roots r₁, r₂, r₃, r₄. Rolle's theorem gives r₁ ≤ d₁ ≤ r₂ ≤ d₂ ≤ r₃ ≤
          d₃ ≤ r₄. With distinct polynomial roots, the inequalities are strict.
          A root repeated m times remains repeated m − 1 times in the
          derivative, explaining equality at the zero-coupling crossing.
        

        

          FiniteFree constructs the exact multivariate determinant, renames its
          context with substitute, and calls
          restrict_line([0,y,z],[1,0,0]) at every rational
          control-grid point. The univariate
          derivative(monic=False) is checked against the restricted
          exact multivariate partial derivative. Embedded strings preserve exact
          coefficients. Browser curves, Jacobi eigenvalues and bracketed
          derivative roots use Float64; the displayed derivative curve is
          divided by 4 for scale.
        

        

          These facts concern this identity-direction pencil; the plots do not
          certify general hyperbolicity or stability. Regenerate with
          PYTHONPATH=. python examples/interactive_geometry.py.
